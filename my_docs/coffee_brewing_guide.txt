The Ultimate Coffee Brewing Guide

Coffee is one of the most popular beverages in the world. The flavor and quality of your cup depend heavily on the brewing method, coffee-to-water ratio, grind size, and water temperature.

BREWING METHODS

1. Pour Over (e.g., Chemex, V60)
   - Grind: Medium-fine
   - Water temp: 90–96°C (195–205°F)
   - Ratio: 1:15 to 1:17 (coffee to water by weight)
   - Brew time: 3–4 minutes
   - Best for: Clean, bright, floral flavors. Highlights origin characteristics.

2. French Press
   - Grind: Coarse
   - Water temp: 93°C (200°F)
   - Ratio: 1:12 to 1:15
   - Brew time: 4 minutes steep, then press
   - Best for: Rich, full-bodied coffee with more oils and texture.

3. Espresso
   - Grind: Very fine
   - Water temp: 90–96°C
   - Pressure: 9 bars
   - Ratio: 1:2 (dose to yield)
   - Brew time: 25–30 seconds
   - Best for: Concentrated shots. Base for lattes, cappuccinos, Americanos.

4. AeroPress
   - Grind: Medium to fine
   - Water temp: 80–96°C
   - Ratio: 1:6 to 1:15
   - Brew time: 1–2 minutes
   - Best for: Versatile and forgiving. Can mimic espresso or filter coffee.

5. Cold Brew
   - Grind: Extra coarse
   - Water temp: Cold or room temperature
   - Ratio: 1:8
   - Brew time: 12–24 hours
   - Best for: Smooth, low-acidity concentrate. Serve over ice.

COFFEE BEANS

- Arabica: Higher quality, complex flavors, lower caffeine. Grown at high altitude.
- Robusta: Stronger, more bitter, higher caffeine. Used in espresso blends and instant coffee.

ROAST LEVELS
- Light roast: Fruity, acidic, preserves origin flavors.
- Medium roast: Balanced sweetness and acidity.
- Dark roast: Bold, bitter, chocolatey, less acidity.

STORAGE
Store coffee beans in an airtight container away from light, heat, and moisture. Avoid the refrigerator. Grind just before brewing for maximum freshness.
