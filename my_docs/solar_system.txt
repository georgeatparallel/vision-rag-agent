The Solar System

The solar system consists of the Sun and all the objects that orbit around it, including eight planets, their moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets.

The eight planets in order from the Sun are:
1. Mercury – the smallest planet and closest to the Sun. It has no atmosphere and extreme temperature swings.
2. Venus – the hottest planet due to its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere and greenhouse effect. It rotates backwards compared to most planets.
3. Earth – the only known planet to harbor life. It has one natural satellite, the Moon, and a protective magnetic field.
4. Mars – known as the Red Planet due to iron oxide on its surface. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, and evidence of ancient liquid water.
5. Jupiter – the largest planet, a gas giant with a Great Red Spot, a storm larger than Earth that has persisted for centuries. It has 95 known moons, including Europa which may have a subsurface ocean.
6. Saturn – famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock. It is the least dense planet and could theoretically float on water.
7. Uranus – an ice giant that rotates on its side with an axial tilt of 98 degrees. It has faint rings and 27 known moons.
8. Neptune – the windiest planet with storms reaching 2,100 km/h. It has a large moon called Triton that orbits in the opposite direction of Neptune's rotation.

The Sun contains 99.86% of the total mass of the solar system. It is a G-type main-sequence star (yellow dwarf) located about 26,000 light-years from the center of the Milky Way galaxy.

The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter and contains millions of rocky objects. The Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune is home to dwarf planets like Pluto, Eris, and Makemake.
